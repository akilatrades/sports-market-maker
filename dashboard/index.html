02 / STRATEGIES

Two ways to be paid for waiting

Both post limit orders and wait to be filled; neither pays the spread. Each was backtested first, then traded with small real orders. Rules below are read from the code on the main branch.

Strategy 1 · Prop maker · live since 19–20 Oct 2026

Resting NO orders on player props

People who buy YES at the ask on NBA and NFL player props are mostly fans backing a player. The bot rests post-only NO orders (they can only wait on the book, never take a price) one cent better than the best bid, or joining it when the spread is 1¢, only where a consensus of two or more sportsbooks with their margin removed (or, failing that, the stat model) values NO above its price.

Edge(NO) = P(NO) − price(NO) − fees

Strategy 2 · Spread maker · live since 22 Oct 2026

Quoting both sides of wide spreads

Thin markets (esports, college and minor-league sports, fights, racing) often trade 8¢ or more apart. The bot bids one cent above the best YES bid and one cent above the best NO bid. If both fill at the same quotes, it owns a pair that pays exactly $1 and cost less; if only one fills, it holds that side to settlement, and a pair filled across a price move can lose. No model, and only before the event starts.

Pair profit = $1 − price(YES) − price(NO) − fees

03 / RESEARCH

What the history shows, including what failed

Full research log ↗

Where did the NO edge appear?

Resting NO orders replayed across Kalshi: net cents per simulated contract after fees, ± 2 standard errors. These are the 15 strongest series with 300+ simulated fills, so they were picked for strength and are biased upward.

A filled dot passed the scan's bar (t ≥ 2, positive in both halves of the sample, and no matching edge on the YES side); a hollow dot did not.

Did not work. Buying the model's side at the ask lost money after fees. Gradient-boosted trees, player effects and recalibration did not improve trading. A second cent of price improvement and early cancellation both cut profit. Selling one-sided spread fills back turned +3.9¢ per contract into roughly break-even, and spread quotes resting during games made +2.7¢ against +9.9¢ before the start.

Limits of the evidence. Every number here is simulated from public trade prints and assumes the order was first in line. Rules were chosen on the same 2025–26 data (in-sample), and fills in the same game move together, so results are judged by game. Live fills and the 2026–27 season are the real test.

04 / RISK CONTROLS

Limits are part of the strategy

The bots stop rather than guess when anything is unclear.

See the tests ↗
Before an order

Fresh odds and model inputs, injury and roster checks, and a fee-aware edge filter. Caps per order, game, day and lifetime.

While it rests

Re-checked every few minutes and cancelled on injury news (ESPN or the NBA's official report), a price or edge change, or stale sportsbook odds. Spread quotes are cancelled before the event starts.

Every pass

Orders, positions and fills are reconciled with the exchange first. Anything unexplained stops every bot until it is reconciled by hand.

One account, shared stops

All bots count toward one daily and one total loss stop, on top of each bot's own limits.
