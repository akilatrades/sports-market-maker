01 / AT A GLANCE

Does a statistical edge survive real trading?

Two strategies, each tested the same way: backtest first, then small real orders. Real fills decide whether either works.

STRATEGY 1 · PROP MAKER · LIVE SINCE 19–20 OCT 2026

Resting NO orders on player props

Traders who buy YES at the ask on NBA and NFL player props are mostly uninformed, so resting NO orders should earn a small edge. Models and sportsbook odds decide which lines to quote. The figures below are this strategy's.

STRATEGY 2 · SPREAD MAKER · LIVE SINCE 22 OCT 2026

Quoting both sides of wide spreads

Smaller markets (esports, college and minor-league sports, fights, racing and more) often trade with a gap of 8¢ or more between bid and offer. Bidding one cent inside on both sides earns most of that gap when both sides fill at those prices. No model: it prices from the order book. How it works ↓

What the terms mean
Order, fill, contract
One order can fill in several pieces (fills); each fill can hold several contracts.
Settled P&L
Contract payout minus entry cost and actual fees, once the market settles.
Volume capture
The share of the volume that traded through an order's price that the order actually received. The backtest assumes 50%.
NO order
A contract that pays $1 if the player does not reach the line. Buying NO is the same as selling the “over”.
Edge
Estimated probability of winning minus the price paid and fees, in cents per $1 contract.

05 / STRATEGY 2

The spread maker

A market maker for thin markets: it never predicts the outcome. When a market's best bid and best offer are 8¢ or more apart, it bids one cent above the best YES bid and one cent above the best NO bid. If both fill at those prices, it owns a YES and a NO that together pay exactly $1 and cost less than $1. If only one fills, it holds that contract to settlement; if the price moves before the other side fills, a pair can also lose.

01 / FIND

Wide spreads, near the end

Every 10 minutes it scans 64 series that passed a false-discovery check, for markets 4–7 hours before expected expiration with a spread of 8¢ or more. NBA and NFL props are left to strategy 1.

Rules ↗
02 / QUOTE

One cent inside, both sides

Post-only bids of up to 5 contracts that expire after 65 minutes. Prices come from the order book with its own orders removed, so it never bids against itself.

Order worker ↗
03 / HOLD

Pairs lock the spread

Both sides filled at the same quotes lock in the spread less 2¢; a pair whose second side fills after the price moved can lose. Single fills are held, never sold back, and at most 5 contracts are held or resting on one side of a market.

Hedging test ↗
04 / MEASURE

Real fills against a shadow

A shadow ledger records what the rule would have filled from public trades. Comparing it with real fills shows how much of the trading at our price we really get.

Pilot plan ↗

Where the evidence comes from. A simulated sweep of nine strategy families across about 200,000 Kalshi markets (September 2025 – August 2026). Two-sided quoting in spreads of 5¢+ was the one new strategy that held up in both halves of the sample (the other two were variations of strategy 1's NO orders); the wider the spread, the larger the edge. About 70% of its simulated profit came from hours in which both sides filled.

Limits of the evidence. Simulated from public trade prints, assuming our order was first in line; the threshold and the 64 series were chosen on the same data. Other traders may already quote inside these spreads. The live pilot, at small size, is the real test.

Does the sportsbook filter help?

Historical fills grouped by the sportsbook filter.

Did not work. Buying the model's side at the ask lost money after fees. Gradient-boosted trees, player effects and recalibration did not improve trading. A second cent of price improvement and early order cancellation both reduced profit. Game-winner markets were flat to negative.

Limits of the evidence. One NBA season, and the rule was chosen on that same season. Historical fills assume the order was first in line. Fills in the same game are correlated, so results are judged by game.

07 / RISK CONTROLS

Limits are part of the strategy

The bots stop rather than guess when anything is unclear.

See the tests ↗
BEFORE AN ORDER

Fresh inputs, fixed limits

Model age, injury and roster checks, and fee-aware edge filters run first. Limits apply per order, game, team, day and lifetime, with daily and total loss stops.

WHILE AN ORDER RESTS

Re-checked every few minutes

An order is cancelled if injury news (ESPN or the NBA's official report), prices or the edge change, or if the sportsbook odds behind it go stale. Every pass first reconciles orders, positions and fills with the exchange.

AFTER

A complete record

Every order is written to a journal before it is sent. Fills, fees and settlements are reconciled, and missing data is reported as missing.

ACROSS BOTH STRATEGIES

One account, shared stops

Each bot owns its markets and recognises the other's orders; anything unexplained stops all of them. Realized losses of every bot count toward one daily and one total loss stop, on top of each bot's own limits.
