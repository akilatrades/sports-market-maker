# NFL evidence review · October 18, 2026

**Decision: retain the small existing caps. Live profitability is unproven.**

The strategy posts passive NO entries and holds them to settlement. It does not
lock in a two-sided spread. A fill proves that someone traded against the quote;
it does not establish that the model's probability was accurate.

The saved NFL comparison contains **231 settled lines from one game** (Seattle
at Denver, October 15). Its older ask-taking paper rule reports 77 one-contract
trades and **-$2.425884**, including -$3.180147 for receiving yards (25 contracts).
These are historical calculations, not account transactions and not a backtest
of the current live maker implementation. Receiving-yard lines cover just 15

The 3¢ requirement removes **67 of 178 marginal lines (37.6%)** in this screen.
These are not proposed orders: the calculation excludes current roster checks,
entry windows, matched sportsbook lines, queue/flow evidence, all sizing caps,
and actual fills. It estimates neither fill rate nor P/L. It cannot prove that
3¢ is optimal. The new threshold is a conservative risk filter.

For a four-contract NO position bought at 66¢, purchase cost is $2.64. Normal
winning settlement returns $4, for +$1.36 before fees; losing settlement returns
$0, for -$2.64 before fees. Its pre-fee break-even win probability is 66%. Under
the new entry rule, reserve($2.64) is $2.91, so the quoted probability must put
the NO outcome at **at least 75.75%** to retain a 3¢ buffered edge per contract.
That is a model admission requirement, not a claim about the true probability.

## What to improve next

1. Reconcile real fees, partial fills and settlements by entry version. Do not
   combine the new cohort with earlier entries or NBA research.
2. Compare post-fill prices and opposing trade flow with queue observations.
   Paying more just to fill can worsen adverse selection. Keep maker-only and
   the bounded second-tick rule; do not chase or blindly cancel/repost.
3. Validate model calibration across independent NFL games, especially receiving
   tails and model-only lines. Review current snaps/routes, quarterback or role
   changes and the point-in-time availability of injury/odds inputs before
   treating the historical prediction score as a live trading edge.
4. Keep the production dashboard separate from the research portfolio. No size
   increase is justified by one filled order or one settled historical game.
